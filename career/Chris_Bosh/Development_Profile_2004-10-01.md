# DEVELOPMENTAL THESIS

Bosh enters his second NBA season as a 20-year-old big whose rookie production has not yet caught up to the sophistication of his skill package.

The intended build is not a traditional interior power forward who gradually develops a jumper.

The intended build is a perimeter-skilled 6'11" forward who gradually becomes strong enough, confident enough and important enough to use the full package at NBA volume.

His long-term offensive identity should be built around four connected abilities:

1. elite shooting efficiency from multiple levels,
2. face-up scoring against slower frontcourt defenders,
3. advanced passing from the elbow and short roll,
4. enough interior scoring and rebounding to punish smaller matchups.

Year 2 is the season where those pieces begin becoming a coherent offensive role.

The long-term destination is not merely a 20-point scorer.

It is a 25-plus point, 11-plus rebound, 4-to-5 assist offensive centerpiece whose efficiency remains unusually high because the defense cannot remove any one part of the floor.

# FREE-THROW INTERPRETATION

For this build, the rookie free-throw percentage should not be treated as Bosh's true shooting ceiling.

It should be treated as an unstable first-year result from a 19-year-old whose mechanics and touch project much higher.

The developmental model should therefore separate:

**Observed Year 1 result:** poor relative to target profile.

from:

**Underlying shooting talent:** elite.

This is essential if the trajectory is intended to produce 90-plus percent free-throw seasons.

Entering Year 2, Bosh should already have undergone substantial mechanical stabilization work during the offseason.

### Year 2 shooting objective

The free-throw line becomes the cleanest statistical evidence that his elite touch is translating.

Target developmental band:

**88 to 91%**

Anything near that range confirms that the rookie percentage was not representative of the underlying skill.

# THREE-POINT DEVELOPMENT

Bosh should not enter Year 2 firing seven threes a game.

The progression needs to be controlled.

### Rookie stage

Open threes are available.

### Year 2 stage

Pick-and-pop threes become a regular part of the offense.

### Year 3 stage

Above-the-break threes become a normal possession outcome rather than a counter.

### Years 4 to 5

Defenders must remain attached to Bosh outside the arc.

### Prime stage

Three-point shooting becomes structural gravity.

The eventual target is approximately four attempts per game while remaining around 40 to 43%.

That volume is large enough to alter defensive positioning without turning Bosh into a perimeter-only player.

# SIGNATURE MOVE PACKAGE - YEAR 2

## 1. PICK-AND-POP CANNON

Primary developmental action.

Bosh screens and immediately creates distance.

The initial Year 2 release points are:

- free-throw line extended,
- elbows,
- short wings,
- selective above-the-break threes.

The long-term goal is forcing opposing centers to defend him 22 to 24 feet from the basket.

---

## 2. ELBOW FACE-UP

Catch around 15 to 18 feet.

Immediate reads:

1. shoot if the defender gives space,
2. attack the front foot,
3. pass to the cutter,
4. reset into handoff or screen action.

This becomes increasingly important every season.

---

## 3. ONE-DRIBBLE PULL-UP

The first major closeout counter.

Bosh does not need a full isolation package if the defender must sprint toward his jumper.

One hard dribble creates enough space.

---

## 4. DOUBLE-SHOULDER TURNAROUND

Still developing.

Bosh should gradually become comfortable turning over either shoulder from the mid-post.

This is a future late-clock tool rather than a Year 2 volume action.

---

## 5. SHORT-ROLL READ

Catch in the middle of the floor after the ball-handler draws two.

Reads:

- finish,
- hit the baseline cutter,
- kick to weak-side corner,
- throw the opposite-slot pass.

This is the action that eventually drives the assist totals above four per game.

---

## 6. TRAIL THREE

Bosh runs into the play behind the initial transition attack.

Instead of automatically entering the paint, he can stop above the break.

This should become increasingly common as his career progresses.

---

## 7. FACE-UP DRIVE

Attack opposing centers from 15 to 20 feet.

Bosh's advantage is the combination of height, touch and first-step quickness rather than elite guard-style handle.

# PRIME ARCHETYPE

At full development, Bosh should function as:

**A 6'11" three-level scoring power forward who rebounds like a traditional big, shoots like a perimeter player, passes like a secondary offensive hub and defends multiple frontcourt actions.**

The mature possession tree becomes:

**Catch outside**

→ shoot  
→ attack closeout  
→ enter pull-up  
→ draw help  
→ make secondary pass

**Catch at elbow**

→ jumper  
→ face-up drive  
→ cutter feed  
→ handoff  
→ high-low

**Screen**

→ pop  
→ roll  
→ short roll  
→ slip  
→ relocate

There should eventually be no standard defensive answer that removes the entire package.
