# Miami's front office

Miami is the only simulated club (world model D). Its front office uses only evidence available on the career date: 2002-03 statistics (`library/2003/league/nba_2003_veteran_ratings.json`), contracts, the 1999 rules and Wade's logged requests. It never reads real-career trajectories or real rosters. All rules are provisional judgement constants in `runtime/front_office.py`.

## June 30, 2003 (roadmap item 4)

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| Team options (Rasual Butler, Sean Lampley, Ken Johnson) | Exercise if the player logged 400+ minutes in 2002-03 or is 24 or younger |
| Qualifying offers (Eddie House, Malik Allen, Mike James) | Tender if 500+ minutes in 2002-03 or 24 or younger; amounts from `00_Team/Finances/free_agent_rights.json` |
| Anthony Carter's player option | His decision, drawn by the engine. Chance of opting in: 0.5 + 0.4 x (option - market value) / option, kept within 5-95%. Market value: his minimum plus a share of the mid-level proportional to 2002-03 minutes (capped at 2,000) |
| Renouncing free agents (Mourning and others) | Not a June 30 decision; it belongs to free-agency planning (item 6) |

Run `python scripts/run_june30.py --write` only when the career clock reaches June 30. It stops if Carter's `years_of_service` is not recorded on `contract_schedules.json` (it sets his minimum salary). It writes the rule decisions with reasons and one `*.decision.json` per chance-based decision; pushing lets Railway draw them, and `/decisions/<event_id>` returns the outcome.

## Miami's holdings (conflict rule 2)

`00_Team/Team/Roster/holdings.json` records, with dates, every player simulated Miami holds: `from` the date Miami holds him, `until` the first date it no longer does (null while held). Real clubs' rotations leave out whoever Miami holds on the game's date. When Miami signs, trades, waives or loses a player, append an entry or set `until` on or after the career date, in the same change as the register; never change a date that has passed, because played games depend on it. On June 26, 2003 the expiring contracts run until July 1, and the pending options stay open until the June 30 decisions close the ones that are declined. Validation checks that every player the register holds is held on its `as_of` date.

## Hiring pool (league staffs)

`library/<year>/league/nba_<season>_staffs.json` (2002-03 to 2013-14, `scripts/import_staffs.py`) lists every real club's head coaches (in order, with the part of the season each coached), top basketball executives and assistant and support staff, from the Basketball-Reference team pages and coaches tables, with no win-loss records. Everyone on a real club's staff on the career date is a candidate Miami can hire; once Miami hires him he leaves his real club from that date. A coaching change counts only once the career clock reaches it, and Miami's own staff from 2003-04 lives in its organization records.

## Wade's requests

Before a decision date, Wade's wishes go in the phase folder's `wade_requests.json`:

```json
{"requests": [{"date": "2003-06-28", "subject": "team_option", "player": "Ken Johnson",
               "requested": "exercise", "note": "what Wade tells the front office"}]}
```

A request that agrees with the rule changes nothing. One that opposes it becomes an engine-drawn decision. The chance it wins is Wade's standing weight times how close the rule's call was (0 = right on the line, 1 = clear-cut). Standing weights (`STANDING_WEIGHT`, one table in `runtime/standing.py`): unsigned rookie 0.15, rookie 0.2, starter 0.35, All-Star 0.6, franchise player 0.8. A clear-cut decision cannot be moved.

## Wade's standing (`runtime/standing.py`)

Standing is computed, never typed: `compute(root, date)` is a pure function of the records on a date and reads only closed simulated results and recorded honors (never the historical Wade). Categories: `unsigned_rookie`, `rookie`, `starter`, `all_star`, `franchise`.

| Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Signed | Wade's entry on `00_Team/Finances/contract_schedules.json` has a `signed_date` on or before the date and its schedule covers the date's season (the signing writes it); unsigned → `unsigned_rookie`. On the career date `contract_status` must agree with the sheet, else the computation refuses |
| Closed seasons | `career/Dwyane_Wade/<season>/season_close.json` with `close_date` on or before the date (written at rollover, roadmap item 18); none → `rookie` |
| Window | the two most recent closed seasons (`EVIDENCE_WINDOW_SEASONS`); standing can fall |
| Starter line | per closed season, from the regular-season games closed by that date: appearances at least half of Miami's scheduled games (`STARTER_GAMES_SHARE`) and starts at least half of appearances (`STARTER_START_SHARE`), or 28 minutes an appearance when starts are unobserved (`STARTER_MINUTES`); a season without complete box coverage gives no evidence, never zero games |
| `franchise` | an MVP or Finals MVP in the window; or All-NBA First or Second Team in the most recent closed season; or any All-NBA team in both seasons of a full window; each with the starter line met in the honor's season |
| `all_star` | an All-Star or any All-NBA honor in the window with the starter line met in its season |
| `starter` | the starter line met in the most recent closed season |
| `rookie` | a signed player below the starter line (the floor; there is no sixth category) |

Statistics alone never reach `all_star`; honors alone never promote (an honor whose season misses the line goes to `basis.conflicts`). Honors are read from `awards.json` by their exact `name` (`docs/player_statistics.md` pins them: `All-Star`, `All-NBA First Team`, `All-NBA Second Team`, `All-NBA Third Team`, `Most Valuable Player`, `Finals MVP`), in the `regular` and `playoff` competitions only (an All-Star selection is filed as `regular`); other names are reported under `basis.ignored_honors`. Until roadmap item 15 writes earned honors, `starter` is the ceiling.

`career/Dwyane_Wade/standing.json` holds append-only dated snapshots `{as_of, standing, trigger, basis, source}`; the drivers read the latest snapshot on or before their date (`standing_on`). A snapshot is written at a signing (`run_free_agency.py`), a season close (rollover) or a recorded honor (item 15), or by hand with `python scripts/update_standing.py --write <date> [--trigger T] [--source REL]`; `--check <date>` prints the computation. A write only dates a snapshot: its value is always the rule's, so nobody can choose a standing. Validation replays every snapshot on its date and refuses a file that is stale against the career date, which is what makes the triggers binding; the file may be absent only while the computed standing is `unsigned_rookie`.

## Franchise consultation (the user's premise)

Once Wade's standing is `franchise`, Miami must ask him before it signs or trades for another star player; the move cannot proceed without his answer, and his objection stops it. Below `franchise` there is no gate and his requests keep their weight. This is a simulation rule chosen by the user, not a CBA right.

| Piece | Rule (`runtime/consultations.py`) |
|---|---|
| Star | 2002-03 production value (`Valuation.value`) of at least `STAR_PRODUCTION_VALUE` 20.0: met by 13 of the June 26 contract inventory in 2002-03 (about one per two clubs; 15.0 would catch 46). A salary prong (38 contracts at 90% of the 0-6-year maximum) was dropped because the inventory carries no years of service, so a tier maximum cannot be computed for trade candidates. A player without 2002-03 evidence (Mourning) is never a star under this rule; the gate uses 2003 evidence only |
| Kinds | acquisitions only: `free_agent` (a plan target), `trade` (the player Miami trades for, or any player arriving in an own sign-and-trade-out), `sign_and_trade` (the player of a sign-and-trade acquisition). Re-signing Miami's own player is keeping, not adding; players Miami sends out are never gated |
| Record | `career/Dwyane_Wade/<season>/Wade_Consultations/<date>-<player>-<kind>.json` (Wade's own decision, never a `*.decision.json`) with the page `Milestone_<date>_consultation_<player>.md` beside it (the player-milestone workflow: identity line, the proposed move, the 2002-03 line and value, the salary or ask, Miami's cap position, fit, the front office's reason, the responses, the next checkpoint). `current_state.json` carries `star_consultation:<id>` in `pending_player_decisions` while unanswered |
| Answers | Wade writes `answer` (`approve` or `object`), `answered` (between the asking date and the career date) and `status: closed` into the record. An objection is kind-agnostic and season-wide: no pursuit of that player by any route until Wade files a new dated record. An approval covers its kind and player for the season; a different kind re-asks. Closed records are never edited |
| The clock | the ask stops the career clock on the asking day for the whole day loop: every other Miami negotiation, draw and signing waits with it, the plan is not made, and a re-run of the same date finds the same ids and writes nothing new. While any consultation is unanswered, whatever day it was asked, the drivers ask nothing else and make no move (`consultations.unanswered`; `ask` returns the open record of the same player and kind rather than writing a second one), so a question is asked once and his answer to it is honored: only an answered record is an answer (`answer_of`), and an open question never hides an earlier answer. The free-agency driver resumes on the next run after the answer (closes the pending entry, opens the approved talks, notes the objection); `run_trade.py --propose` writes the consultation instead of the proposal and makes no other proposal that day (the stall is deliberate: the second-best proposal would usually spend the same outgoing players), and `--propose`/`--shop` on any later day report the open question and write nothing |
| Binding | the gate binds at proposal time: a drawn acceptance is applied without re-asking. Validation (`consultation_errors`) checks the schema, the ids, the page, the asking date against the career clock, the answer dates, the standing on the record, the pending list and that no player has two unanswered questions |

## Trades (roadmap item 16, phase C of `front_office_design.md`)

`python scripts/run_trade.py --search <date>` ranks the proposals Miami's front office would make on the date; `--propose <date>` writes the top-ranked one to `00_Team/Transactions/Trades/<id>.json` with the real club's acceptance as a decision packet beside it; `--write <date>` applies drawn answers on or after the date; `--shop <date>` lets the front office shop its own agreed, still unsigned free agents as sign-and-trades signed and traded in one transaction (it takes no player: the front office picks; a player Miami has already re-signed is a signed player under the newly-signed restriction and is not shopped). The front office chooses the proposal; the user only triggers the step. ## Fortnightly staff rotation reviews

`python scripts/review_rotation.py --write <date>` processes reviews every fourteen days after the camp rotation's `as_of` date, through the final regular-season game. For an October 24 camp decision, the first reviews are November 7 and November 21. The command cannot pass the career clock, advance it, or play games. `--check <date>` reports due work and validates existing reviews without writing.

Before each review, every earlier scheduled Miami game must have a played note declaring its terminated result, or a canceled note with a reason. Raw result sidecars and games on or after the review day are excluded. The staff freezes each source path and hash alongside its assessment.

The rating uses NBA efficiency—points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, less missed field goals, missed free throws and turnovers—per 30 minutes. Each player's fixed camp staff estimate contributes 300 prior minutes. The score is `(preseason estimate × 300 + closed efficiency × 30) / (300 + closed minutes)`. Every review starts from that same preseason prior, so earlier reviews do not count the evidence twice. A player with 30 closed minutes gets 9.1% weight on those results; at 300 minutes the weights are equal. New arrivals without a camp score need a dated staff estimate in `Depth_Chart/preseason_estimates.json`, with `players` entries containing `player`, `as_of` and `score`.

At each primary position, a clear leader takes the starting job. If the top two scores differ by at most 10% of their absolute scale (with a floor of one), the engine draws the winner. A tie is 50–50; the leader's probability rises to 75% at the threshold. Eddie Jones has no incumbent or seniority bonus; Wade has no draft-slot or protagonist bonus in these reviews. Their frozen preseason assessments are treated by the same formula as everyone else's.

Records live under `00_Team/Team/Depth_Chart/Reviews/YYYY-MM-DD/`: immutable `review.json`, battle `*.decision.json` requests and checked engine answers, then `depth_chart.json` and a 240-minute `rotation.json`. Submit pending battle requests through the existing authorized engine decision workflow and run the review command again after collection. No rotation is completed until every required answer is present. The original camp files remain the baseline; subsequent review files select the rotation in force for each new game.

An injured starter's healthy depth-chart replacement is explicitly marked as a starter in the game request. The engine's actual opening-five flags feed starts in reports and the established season-close standing rule. A later staff decision cannot rewrite a played game's lineup or start credit.

## Wade's rookie contract (roadmap item 5)

Miami opens with 120% of the No. 5 scale, the customary level for first-round picks: $2,636,400, $2,834,160 and $3,031,920 over 2003-04 to 2005-06, plus a $3,841,443 team option for 2006-07, to be exercised by October 31, 2005 (October 31 after his second season, FAQ Q38). Miami intends to sign him after its July free-agency moves, because until he signs he counts at 100% of scale.

**Wade's standing counter (user premise, October 3, 2026).** `Wade_Rookie_Contract/standing_instruction.json` holds Wade's pre-registered answer: 80% of scale as protected Current Cash Compensation plus incentives to 120%, 20% in incentives the 1999 rules count as Salary (a designated offseason skill-and-conditioning program and an objective preseason conditioning standard, 10% each) and 20% in performance incentives the league classifies (Unlikely for a rookie: 1,200 regular-season minutes; All-Rookie selection in 2003-04, then 15.0 and 17.0 points a game in at least 60 games), no signing bonus, the promised role a non-contractual representation, the fourth-year option at 26.7% over the third season's counted Salary. `runtime/rookie_contract.layered_terms` builds it from the scale and `layered_errors` checks the rules (80% protected floor, 120% ceiling, Unlikely bonuses at most 25% of Regular Salary). The driver submits the counter the day Miami offers, Miami answers by rule the next day (a legal counter within its offered ceiling with a lower protected floor is agreed), Wade's acceptance follows from the instruction, and execution waits for the calendar's first legal signing day (July 16, 2003; the user's source said August 1, which the verified calendar does not support). The cap sheet counts protected cash plus included incentives plus bonuses classified Likely; each season's incentives are evaluated against closed results at rollover (roadmap item 18).

The free-agency driver opens `01_Free_Agency/Wade_Rookie_Contract/negotiation_log.json` with this offer and a role promise once Miami's July talks are closed (`python scripts/open_rookie_negotiation.py --write <date>` does the same by hand). Wade's answers (accept, counter, request a signing date, hold out) are the user's decisions and are appended as entries. Validation checks every entry's terms against the rookie scale and keeps dates in order; nothing may follow the signing.

## Minimum salaries

Every minimum offer uses the 1999 CBA minimum for the player's years of NBA service before the season (`runtime/cba.minimum_salary`, `library/2003/league/nba_1999_cba_minimum_salary_scale.json`), one figure per year of service. A veteran whose service is not recorded is not offered a minimum (`Valuation.signing_minimum`); an undrafted player with no NBA history gets the rookie minimum. A one-year minimum for a player with five or more years counts only the four-year minimum in team salary (`cap_amount` on the contract schedule; the league reimburses the rest). The November 11, 2003 correction of the earlier camp and refill minimums is recorded in `00_Team/Finances/minimum_salary_corrections.json` (`scripts/correct_minimum_salaries.py`).

`finance.json` and the projection in `contract_schedules.json` are rebuilt from the live ledger on every transaction (`signing.refresh_aggregates`), and validation reconciles them at every date after June 26.
